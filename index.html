The book

In 1843 Sarah Tucker Bucknall left Stroud in Gloucestershire, sailed four months with her husband and her children, and began again on unbroken country in the Port Phillip District.

She raised eight children, buried some of them, and built Rodborough Vale. Her husband's name is on the memorial. Hers is on almost nothing at all.

Sarah of Rodborough is drawn from surviving family letters, court records and photographs, and follows Sarah across fifty years — England to the colony, the diggings, the drought, and the long distances between a mother and the children the world scattered.

Two lines, one ship

Harriet Martin sailed as the Bucknalls' servant. More than a century later, both women turn out to be ancestors of the same descendant — the author.

The mistress and the maid, on the same passenger list, both grandmothers of the woman writing this.

The author

Di Williams is the founder and Chair of Fernwood Fitness and the author of three works of non-fiction. Sarah of Rodborough is her first novel. Sarah Tucker Bucknall was her great-great-grandmother.
